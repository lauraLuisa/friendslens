The companion that sees everyone: friendslens and the privacy question

3 Mins Read

Elian Nova

Author

Nov 20, 2040

Friendslens Labs promises that its users will never walk into a conversation alone. Privacy advocates say that is exactly the problem: every conversation a wearer has now includes a third party that nobody else agreed to.

The Companion Lens, launched this year with Companion OS 7, pairs two AI contact lenses with a neural headpiece. The lenses read the emotions and body language of whoever the wearer is talking to. The headpiece gives you social advice and can release a targeted dose of dopamine to stimulate your brain in difficult situations. The hardware is free. Users pay from €9.99 a month for companion time.

Read without asking

The sharpest criticism concerns bystanders. The lenses analyse every face in view and label people as "open", "needs support" or "busy". They also record key moments so the wearer can replay them later. None of the people being read are asked for consent, and most will never know.

"You can say no to a camera. You cannot say no to someone's eyes," said Mara Velten of the digital rights group Open Faces. "A colleague at lunch is now a data subject without being told."

The company's own quick guide admits the gap. It says the lenses read and record the people a wearer talks to, and that "they have not agreed to it". It then asks wearers to be honest if someone asks. Critics note that this puts the duty on the user, not the product.

A device that knows your brain

The wearer pays an even higher price. During a 7-day calibration phase, the companion learns the user's voice and emotions, the people who matter to them and the situations that trigger their anxiety. The headpiece also connects directly to the brain and releases a personal dose of dopamine when needed.

That makes friendslens one of the most intimate data collectors on the market. "This is a mental health profile, a social graph and a neurochemical record in one place," said data protection lawyer Jonas Brenner. "And it is attached to a subscription that earns more the longer you stay connected."

The pricing model adds to the concern. Users get four hours a day for €9.99, with each extra hour costing €2 a month. The company's own leaflet lists booking more companion time than planned as a possible side effect.

What the company says

Friendslens Labs did not answer detailed questions about where emotion data and recordings are stored, or for how long. Consumer groups are now calling for clear rules: a visible signal whenever the lenses read someone, a ban on recording bystanders and full transparency about what happens to the data. Until then, every friendslens user brings an invisible guest to the table.
